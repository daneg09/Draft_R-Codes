## Element objectives `O2.7`: Building separation Element Objectives

Development is to achieve the following Element Objectives: O 2.7.1 New development supports the desired future streetscape character with spaces between buildings. O 2.7.2 Building separation is in proportion to building height. O 2.7.3 Buildings are separated sufficiently to provide for residential amenity including visual and acoustic privacy, natural ventilation, sunlight and daylight access and outlook. O 2.7.4 Suitable areas are provided for communal and private open space, deep soil areas and landscaping between buildings.

Source p22.

## Questions to ask the applicant

- `building.separation_within_site_m`: What is the separation within site? (m; separation between buildings or wings within the site (Table 2.7))
- `building.separation_storeys`: What is the separation storeys? (storey band of the facing walls for Table 2.7)
- `building.same_lot_separation_applies`: The lot holds more than one building, or a building has portions that face each other (C3.4.6 condition)? (yes/no)
- `building.separation_to_boundary_m`: What is the separation to boundary? (m; separation to the adjoining property boundary (Table 2.7))
- `adjoining.r_code`: What is the r code? (code; R-coding of the adjoining property on the boundary the building faces, absent for uncoded land (Table 2.7: 9 m to R60 or below, 6 m to R80 or above; key of table-2.7-adjoining))

## Provisions

### `A2.7.1` Building separation within the site at least the Table 2.7 distance ⚑

Facts: `building.separation_within_site_m`, `building.separation_storeys`, `building.same_lot_separation_applies`.

Kind measurable; column A; coding R80+,LR1,MR1,MR2,HR1,HR2,HR3,R-PA,LR,MR,HR; applies to multiple_dwelling; source p22 Table 2.7.

> A 2.7.1 Development complies with the separation requirements set out in Table 2.7.

Test: building.separation_within_site_m >= Table 2.7 by separation_storeys.
- Table 2.7 (Table 2.7 Building separation within site boundary): separation_storeys <= 4: 3 m (up to 4 storeys); separation_storeys >= 5: 6 m (5 storeys and above).
Applies only when: building.same_lot_separation_applies = yes.
Not met: assess against `O2.7`.
Defined terms:
- *storey* (A1 p10): the portion of a building which is situated between the top of any floor and the top of the floor next above it and if there is no floor above it, that portion between the top of the floor and the ceiling above it but does not include: (i) a basement; (ii) a space that contains only a lift shaft, stairway or meter room; (iii) roof top terrace with no permanent impervious roof structure; (iv) a mezzanine; or (v) a loft. Double height floors greater than 5m floor to ceiling are counted as two floors.
⚑ Verification note: Table 2.7 (p22) heads its columns 'Building height: Up to 4 storeys / 5 storeys and above' without saying whose height (the taller facing building or each facing portion).

## Design guidance `DG2.7`: Building separation Design Guidance

DG 2.7.1 Where the Acceptable Outcomes cannot be achieved due to site constraints, screening, window offsetting, or raised sills should be used to achieve an appropriate level of privacy, while maintaining outlook, solar access and natural ventilation. DG 2.7.2 Required separation between towers should increase alongside defined building height thresholds to manage privacy and outlook. DG 2.7.3 Building separation should consider the quality of outlook from habitable rooms, not solely against direct line-of-sight overlooking. Achieving the separation distances while facing a blank wall is a poor amenity outcome, and may not achieve the Element Objectives. Separation between buildings on the same site should be wide enough to avoid compromising natural cross-ventilation to apartments on either side. Figure 2.7a: Building separation is measured from the outer face of building envelopes which includes balconies.

Source p23.
