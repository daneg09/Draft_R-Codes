## Element objectives `O4.1`: Solar and daylight access element objectives

O 4.1.1 In climate zones 4, 5 and 6: the development is sited and designed to optimise the number of dwellings receiving winter sunlight to private open space and via windows to habitable rooms. O 4.1.2 Windows are designed and positioned to optimise daylight access for habitable rooms. O 4.1.3 The development incorporates shading and glare control to minimise heat gain and glare: from mid-spring to autumn in climate zones 4, 5 and 6 AND year-round in climate zones 1 and 3.

Source p50.

## Questions to ask the applicant

- `site.climate_zone`: What is the climate zone? (NCC climate zone (4, 5, 6 trigger overshadowing))
- `dwellings.with_2h_sun_pct`: What is the with 2h sun? (%; share of dwellings with 2 hours of direct sun (C2.2.2, 70%))
- `dwellings.no_sun_pct`: What is the no sun? (%; share with no direct sun (C2.2.2, max 15%))
- `dwellings.habitable_rooms_external_window_visible`: Every habitable room has at least one window in an external wall, visible from all parts of the room (A4.1.2)? (yes/no)
- `dwellings.min.habitable_window_glazing_pct`: What is the min habitable window glazing? (%; lowest glazed area of any habitable room's windows as % of that room's internal floor area (A4.1.2, 10%))
- `dwellings.min.habitable_window_clear_glazing_pct`: What is the min habitable window clear glazing? (%; lowest share of that glazing that is clear glazing (A4.1.2, 50%))
- `dwellings.lightwell_skylight_primary_daylight`: Any habitable room takes its primary daylight from a lightwell or skylight (A4.1.3)? (yes/no)

## Provisions

### `A4.1.1` Climate zones 4 to 6: at least 70% of dwellings get 2 hours of sun to living rooms and private open space, and at most 15% in a building get none

Facts: `site.climate_zone`, `dwellings.with_2h_sun_pct`, `dwellings.no_sun_pct`.

Kind measurable; column A; coding R80+,LR1,MR1,MR2,HR1,HR2,HR3,R-PA,LR,MR,HR; applies to multiple_dwelling; source p50.

> In climate zones 4, 5 and 6 only: (a) Dwellings with a northern aspect are maximised, with a minimum of 70 per cent of dwellings having living rooms and private open space that obtain at least 2 hours direct sunlight between 9am and 3pm on 21 June AND (b) A maximum of 15 per cent of dwellings in a building receiving no direct sunlight between 9am and 3pm on 21 June.

Test: (dwellings.with_2h_sun_pct >= 70% AND dwellings.no_sun_pct <= 15%).
Applies only when: site.climate_zone >= 4; site.climate_zone <= 6.
Not met: assess against `O4.1`.
Defined terms:
- *climate zone* (A1 p4): as defined by the NCC. Western Australia is divided into five climate zones based on humidity and temperature, ranging from temperate in the southwest to hot/arid in the interior to hot/humid in the north. Refer Climate Zones Map.
- *habitable room/space* (A1 p6): as defined by the NCC for a room/space used for normal domestic activities that includes: (i) a bedroom, living room, lounge room, music room, sitting room, television room, kitchen, scullery, dining room, sewing room, study, playroom, family room, sunroom, gymnasium, fully enclosed swimming pool or patio; but excludes: (i) a bathroom, laundry, water closet, pantry, walk-in wardrobe, corridor, hallway, lobby, photographic darkroom, clothes drying room, verandah and unenclosed swimming pool or patio and other spaces of a specialised nature occupied neither frequently nor for extended periods.
- *private open space* (A1 p8): means outdoor space located at ground level or on a structure that is within private ownership and provided for the recreational use of residents of the dwelling to which it abuts and excludes car parking spaces and access ways. It includes primary garden areas, outdoor living areas, balconies, courtyards and terraces.

### `A4.1.2` Every habitable room has an external-wall window visible from all of the room, glazed area at least 10% of floor area, at least 50% clear glazing

Facts: `dwellings.habitable_rooms_external_window_visible`, `dwellings.min.habitable_window_glazing_pct`, `dwellings.min.habitable_window_clear_glazing_pct`.

Kind measurable; column A; coding R80+,LR1,MR1,MR2,HR1,HR2,HR3,R-PA,LR,MR,HR; applies to multiple_dwelling; source p50.

> Every habitable room has at least one window in an external wall, visible from all parts of the room, with a glazed area not less than 10 per cent of the internal floor area and comprising a minimum of 50 per cent of clear glazing.

Test: (dwellings.habitable_rooms_external_window_visible = yes AND dwellings.min.habitable_window_glazing_pct >= 10% AND dwellings.min.habitable_window_clear_glazing_pct >= 50%).
Not met: assess against `O4.1`.
Defined terms:
- *internal floor area* (A1 p6): in relation to a room or dwelling, the internal area measured within the finished surfaces of the walls, and includes the area occupied by any cupboard or other built-in furniture, fixture or fitting but excludes any garages.

### `A4.1.3` Lightwells and skylights are not the primary daylight source to any habitable room

Facts: `dwellings.lightwell_skylight_primary_daylight`.

Kind conditional; column A; coding R80+,LR1,MR1,MR2,HR1,HR2,HR3,R-PA,LR,MR,HR; applies to multiple_dwelling; source p50.

> Lightwells and/or skylights do not form the primary source of daylight to any habitable room.

Test: dwellings.lightwell_skylight_primary_daylight = no.
Not met: assess against `O4.1`.

### `A4.1.4` Orientation and external shading limit summer sun to habitable rooms and permit winter sun per A4.1.1(a)

Facts: none (judgment against the text).

> The building is oriented and incorporates external shading devices in order to: minimise direct sunlight to habitable rooms: between late September and early March in climate zones 4, 5 and 6 only AND in all seasons in climate zones 1 and 3; permit winter sun to habitable rooms in accordance with A 4.1.1 (a).

## Design guidance `DG4.1`: Solar and daylight access design guidance

DG 4.1.1 In cooler climates south facing dwellings that receive no direct sunlight should be minimised. In tropical areas winter solar gain is less critical, however winter sun access on cooler dry mornings can still be beneficial. A more important design consideration in tropical areas is shading to southern and western elevations to protect against mid-summer sun. DG 4.1.2 Minimum requirements for access to sunlight for habitable rooms and private open space is measured at mid-winter (21 June) when passive heating is most beneficial. Providing direct sunlight to dwellings may be optimised through a number of design features such as: dual aspect apartments; shallow apartment layouts; two storey and mezzanine level apartments; bay windows. DG 4.1.3 Consider strategies to maximise solar access to ground floor apartments such as: high ceilings and tall windows; trees and shrubs that allow solar access in winter and shade in summer. DG 4.1.4 For constrained sites where east-west facing dwellings cannot be avoided, alternative design measures should be implemented to enhance natural light penetration and daylight performance. Such measures may include increased floor-to-ceiling glazing, high-performance glazing and internal design solutions. DG 4.1.5 Daylight access is optimised when windows are visible from all parts of a habitable room and the window has direct access to the sky, rather than being within a deep façade, with overhangs above. Daylight access can also be enhanced through the use of reflected light via light shelves, light coloured internal finishes and higher ceilings. However care must be taken to avoid unwanted glare effects. DG 4.1.6 Where there are restrictions on the use of clear glazing to manage impacts on visual privacy, consider design options such as: increasing ceiling heights to increase the overall height of the window and light reflectance from the ceiling; using a window with a lower sill and providing obscure glazing to 1.6m with clear glazing above. DG 4.1.7 In climate zones 4, 5 and 6, shading from summer sun is most required between November to March. Consider pairing shading treatments with high performance glazing and the insulation of external walls to reduce heat transfer into the dwelling.

Source p51.
