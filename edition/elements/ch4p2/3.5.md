## Element objectives `O3.5`: Visual privacy Element Objectives

O 3.5.1 The orientation and design of buildings, windows and balconies minimises direct overlooking of habitable rooms and private outdoor living areas within the site and of neighbouring properties, while maintaining daylight and solar access, ventilation and the external outlook of habitable rooms.

Source p36.

## Questions to ask the applicant

- `adjoining.vacant_or_unknown`: Adjoining site vacant or dwelling layout unknown (C3.10.4)? (yes/no)
- `privacy.bedroom_study_setback_m`: What is the bedroom study setback? (m; minimum cone-of-vision setback from a bedroom or study major opening)
- `privacy.cone_captures_adjoining_opening`: Cone captures a major opening or active habitable space of an existing adjoining dwelling? (yes/no)
- `privacy.sources_above_0_5m`: Any major opening or active habitable space with floor level more than 0.5 m above NGL? (yes/no)
- `privacy.other_habitable_setback_m`: What is the other habitable setback? (m; from other habitable room major openings)
- `privacy.outdoor_active_setback_m`: What is the outdoor active setback? (m; from outdoor active habitable spaces)
- `dwellings.balconies_provided`: Any apartment has a balcony (C1.1.2 condition)? (yes/no)
- `dwellings.min.balcony_unscreened_perimeter_pct`: What is the min balcony unscreened perimeter? (%; lowest % of balcony perimeter unscreened (C1.1.2))
- `privacy.living_room_outlook`: A3.5.4? (yes/no)

## Provisions

### `A3.5.1-r1` Cone of vision setback from a bedroom, study or open access walkway major opening, first 4 storeys: 3 m (Table 3.5)

Facts: `adjoining.vacant_or_unknown`, `privacy.bedroom_study_setback_m`, `privacy.cone_captures_adjoining_opening`, `privacy.sources_above_0_5m`.

Kind measurable; column A; coding R80+,LR1,MR1,MR2,HR1,HR2,HR3,R-PA,LR,MR,HR; applies to multiple_dwelling; source p36 Table 3.5.

> For development adjoining an existing dwelling, all sources of overlooking are orientated, offset or setback in accordance with Table 3.5 so that the cone of vision (refer to Figure 3.5a) does not capture major openings and/or active habitable spaces on an adjoining property (refer to Figure 3.5b).

Test: privacy.bedroom_study_setback_m >= 3.0 m.
Applies only when: privacy.sources_above_0_5m = yes; adjoining.vacant_or_unknown = no.
Exception 1: cone of vision does not capture a major opening or active habitable space of the adjoining existing dwelling. When privacy.cone_captures_adjoining_opening = no: the provision does not apply.
Not met: assess against `O3.5`.
Defined terms:
- *cone of vision* (A1 p5): the limits of outlook from any given viewpoint for the purposes of assessing the extent of overlooking from that point, measured by applying a viewing cut off angle not less than 45 degrees horizontally and extending 90 degrees down to the ground.
- *major opening* (A1 p7): a window, door or other opening in the exterior wall of a habitable room that provides external means of light or view for that room or space, but does not include an opening or openings that: (i) in aggregate do not exceed 1m² in any such wall, (provided that adjoining or contiguous windows at the junction of two walls forming an internal angle of 90 degrees or less shall be aggregated); or (ii) are glazed in an obscure material and are not able to be opened; or (iii) have a sill height not less than 1.6m above floor level.
- *active habitable space* (A1 p4): any: (i) habitable room with a internal floor area greater than 10m²; and (ii) balcony, courtyard, patio, verandah or other forms of private open space.
- *adjoining property* (A1 p4): any lot: (i) on which any dwelling for which provision is made in the R-Codes may be constructed under the scheme; and (ii) which shares a boundary or portion of a boundary with a lot on which there is a proposed residential development site or is separated from that lot by a right-of-way, vehicle access way, pedestrian access way, access leg of a battleaxe lot or the equivalent not more than 6m in width.
- *natural ground level* (A1 p7): the levels on a site which precede the proposed development, excluding any site works unless approved by the decision-maker or established as part of subdivision of the land preceding development.

## Design guidance `DG3.5`: Visual privacy Design Guidance

DG 3.5.1 For people to feel comfortable living in higher-density environments, the siting of buildings and the design of apartments should carefully balance the need for outlook with the need for privacy. DG 3.5.2 Document the location of existing windows and balconies in adjoining buildings during the site analysis phase and endeavour to offset new habitable room windows and balconies from existing ones. DG 3.5.3 Retaining an open aspect from balconies and principal windows in interior rooms is critical for resident amenity. Screens, enclosures, highlight windows, or landscape elements should not be used to justify a reduction in the visual privacy setbacks for the primary outlook from interior spaces. While balconies can increase internal privacy they may also restrict daylight access. DG 3.5.4 Bedrooms, living spaces and other habitable rooms should be separated from gallery walkway access and other open circulation space by the apartment's service areas or lightwells. DG 3.5.5 Communal open space, common areas and access paths should be separated from private open space and apartment windows, particularly to habitable rooms. Design solutions may include: increased setbacks; solid or partially-solid balustrades to balconies at lower levels; use of fencing and/or trees and vegetation to separate spaces; screening devices; changes of level; bay windows or pop out windows to direct outlook away from other dwellings; planter boxes incorporated into walls and balustrades; pergolas or shading devices to limit overlooking of lower dwellings or private open space; portions of fixed louvres or screen panels to windows and balconies.

Source p37.
