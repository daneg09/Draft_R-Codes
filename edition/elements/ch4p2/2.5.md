Not met: assess against `O2.5`.
Defined terms:
- *plot ratio* (A1 p8): the ratio of the gross plot ratio area of buildings on a development site to the area of land in the site boundaries.
- *plot ratio area* (A1 p8): the gross total area of all floors of buildings on a development site, including the area of any internal and external walls but not including: (i) the areas of any lift shafts; (ii) stairs or stair landings common to two or more dwellings; (iii) machinery, air conditioning and equipment rooms; (iv) space that is wholly below natural ground level; (v) area used exclusively for the parking of wheeled vehicles at or below natural ground level; (vi) storerooms; (vii) lobbies, bin storage areas, passageways to bin storage areas or amenities areas common to more than one dwelling; and (viii) balconies, eaves, verandahs, courtyards and roof terraces.

## Design guidance `DG2.5`: Plot ratio Design Guidance

DG 2.5.1 Meeting the Acceptable Outcome for plot ratio alone does not ensure good amenity and should always considered alongside height, setbacks, building separation, and solar access Design Elements. Plot ratio is intended to operate as one of several complementary controls that collectively influence development intensity, building bulk and built form outcomes. DG 2.5.2 Where plot ratio is high, achieving the Element Objective may involve slender, well-separated built form rather than maximising site coverage at every level. Redistribution of plot ratio floor space above the relevant building height acceptable outcome may be appropriate under certain circumstances to protect the amenity of adjoining properties or even properties further afield (i.e. overshadowing). This is because plot ratio assists in managing how floor area is distributed within the available building envelope, reducing the need to rely solely on height, setback and building separation controls to influence building bulk. DG 2.5.3 Distinguishing commercial/retail podium floor space from residential floor space in plot ratio calculations helps ensure mixed-use outcomes do not compromise residential amenity above.

Source p20.
