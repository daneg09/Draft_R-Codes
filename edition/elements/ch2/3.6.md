## Merit assessment `M3.6`: Streetscape merit assessment criteria

M3.6.1 The design of dwelling façades, street walls and fences in the street setback area contributes positively to streetscape, context and local character. M3.6.2 The building design addresses street frontages and provides opportunity for passive surveillance and social interaction. M3.6.3 Dwelling and building entries are: i. accessible and protected from the weather; and ii. well-lit for safety and amenity, without opportunity for concealment, and designed to enable passive surveillance of the entry from within the lot. M3.6.4 Visual connectivity between the dwelling and the streetscape should be maintained and the effect of the garage on the streetscape should be minimised whereby the streetscape is not dominated by garages. M3.6.5 The height of street walls and fences allows for passive surveillance of the street from the development whilst balancing the need for privacy of private open space and the impact of traffic noise, where located on a primary distributor, district distributor or integrator arterial road.

Source p37.

## Provisions

### `C3.6.1` Dwelling addresses the street

Facts: `building.entrance_identifiable_from_street`, `building.major_opening_to_frontage`.

Kind conditional; column C; coding all; applies to single_house; source p37.

> The dwelling is to address the street (including a battleaxe driveway, communal street or right-of-way where this is the primary frontage) in accordance with the following: i. the primary entrance to the dwelling must be readily identifiable from the street; and ii. provide at least one major opening on the dwelling frontage with an outlook to the street.

Test: (building.entrance_identifiable_from_street = yes AND building.major_opening_to_frontage = yes).
Not met: assess against `M3.6`.
Defined terms:
- *major opening* (A1 p7): a window, door or other opening in the exterior wall of a habitable room that provides external means of light or view for that room or space, but does not include an opening or openings that: (i) in aggregate do not exceed 1m² in any such wall, (provided that adjoining or contiguous windows at the junction of two walls forming an internal angle of 90 degrees or less shall be aggregated); or (ii) are glazed in an obscure material and are not able to be opened; or (iii) have a sill height not less than 1.6m above floor level.
- *primary street* (A1 p8): unless otherwise designated by the local government, the sole or principal public road that provides access to the major entry (front door) to the dwelling or building.
- *frontage* (A1 p5): the width of a lot at the primary street setback line, provided that in the case of battleaxe or other irregularly shaped lots, it shall be as determined by the decision-maker.
- *communal street* (A1 p4): common property or private street providing joint access to two or more dwellings in a residential development and may include vehicle and pedestrian access, and landscaping.
- *right-of-way* (A1 p8): a strip of land such as a laneway, available either for use by the general public, or a restricted section of the community, and may be created by subdivision, specific transfer, or continued use over a period of years. They may be: (i) private, where the land is created in a plan of subdivision and available to landowners that are legally entitled; or (ii) public, where the land has been vested in the Crown.

### `C3.6.2` Upper-level outlook to adjoining public open space

Facts: `building.upper_level_outlook_to_pos`, `proposal.storeys`, `site.adjoins_public_open_space`.

Kind conditional; column C; coding all; applies to single_house; source p37.

> Where adjoining public open space, dwellings with two or more storeys are to include an upper-level balcony and/or window to a habitable room which overlooks the public open space.

Test: building.upper_level_outlook_to_pos = yes.
Applies only when: site.adjoins_public_open_space = yes; proposal.storeys >= 2.
Not met: assess against `M3.6`.
Defined terms:
- *public open space* (A1 p8): publicly accessible land set aside for the purpose of public enjoyment and protection of unique, environmental, social and cultural values for existing and future generations. It is vested in or under the control of a public authority.
- *habitable room/space* (A1 p6): as defined by the NCC for a room/space used for normal domestic activities that includes: (i) a bedroom, living room, lounge room, music room, sitting room, television room, kitchen, scullery, dining room, sewing room, study, playroom, family room, sunroom, gymnasium, fully enclosed swimming pool or patio; but excludes: (i) a bathroom, laundry, water closet, pantry, walk-in wardrobe, corridor, hallway, lobby, photographic darkroom, clothes drying room, verandah and unenclosed swimming pool or patio and other spaces of a specialised nature occupied neither frequently nor for extended periods.
- *balcony* (A1 p4): a balustraded platform on the outside of a dwelling with access from an upper internal room.

### `C3.6.3` Garage width as a proportion of frontage ⚑

Facts: `parking.garage.entrance_clearly_visible`, `parking.garage.ext_width_m`, `parking.garage.present`, `parking.garage.upper_floor_over_half_width`, `site.frontage_m`.

Kind measurable; column C; coding all; applies to single_house; source p37.

> A garage door and its supporting structures (or a garage wall where a garage is aligned parallel to the street) facing the primary street is not to occupy more than 50 per cent of the frontage at the setback line as viewed from the street. This may be increased up to 60 per cent where an upper floor balcony extends for more than half the width of the garage and its supporting structures (or a garage wall where a garage is aligned parallel to the street) and the entrance to the dwelling is clearly visible from the primary street (refer Figure 3.6a).

Test: parking.garage.ext_width_m as % of site.frontage_m <= 50%.
Applies only when: parking.garage.present = yes.
Exception 1: upper floor or balcony over more than half the garage width and entrance clearly visible. When (parking.garage.upper_floor_over_half_width = yes AND parking.garage.entrance_clearly_visible = yes): sets value = 60.
Not met: assess against `M3.6`.
Defined terms:
- *garage* (A1 p5): any roofed structure, other than a carport, designed to accommodate one or more motor vehicles.
- *street setback line* (A1 p10): the minimum distance between the street boundary and a building in accordance with Part B, Table B and Part C, Table 3.3a.
⚑ Verification note: Page internally inconsistent: the clause reads 'an upper floor balcony extends for more than half the width of the garage' while Figure 3.6a reads 'upper floor or balcony'. Encoded on the figure reading (parking.garage.upper_floor_over_half_width). Values 50% / 60% confirmed.

### `C3.6.4` Carport width as a proportion of frontage ⚑

Facts: `parking.carport.present`, `parking.carport.width_m`, `site.frontage_m`.

Kind measurable; column C; coding all; applies to single_house; source p37.

> Carports and supporting structure shall not exceed 60 per cent of the frontage where projected forward of the primary street setback line in accordance with C3.3.4.

Test: parking.carport.width_m as % of site.frontage_m <= 60%.
Applies only when: parking.carport.present = yes.
Not met: assess against `M3.6`.
Defined terms:
- *carport* (A1 p4): An unenclosed structure designed to accommodate one or more motor vehicles and being without a door.
⚑ Verification note: 60% value and wording confirmed. The trigger 'where projected forward of the primary street setback line in accordance with C3.3.4' is not encoded (no fact); the rule runs for any carport, so it can report DOES_NOT_MEET for a carport behind the setback line.

### `C3.6.5(i)` Front fence or wall maximum height 1.8 m

Facts: `building.front_fence.height_m`, `building.front_fence.present`.

Kind measurable; column C; coding numeric,LR,MR,HR,LR1,MR1,MR2,HR1,HR2,HR3; applies to single_house; source p38.

> When provided, fences or walls within the primary street setback area are to be: i. a maximum height of 1.8m; and ii. visually permeable above 1.2m (refer Figure 3.6b); iii. measured from natural ground level on the primary street side of the fence or wall.

Test: building.front_fence.height_m <= 1.8 m.
Applies only when: building.front_fence.present = yes.
Not met: assess against `M3.6`.
Defined terms:
- *natural ground level* (A1 p7): the levels on a site which precede the proposed development, excluding any site works unless approved by the decision-maker or established as part of subdivision of the land preceding development.
- *visually permeable* (A1 p10): in reference to a wall, gate, door, screen or fence that the vertical surface, when viewed directly from the street or other public space, has: (i) continuous vertical or horizontal gaps of 50mm or greater width occupying not less than one third of the total surface area; (ii) continuous vertical or horizontal gaps less than 50mm in width, occupying at least one half of the total surface area in aggregate; or (iii) a surface offering equal or lesser obstruction to view.
